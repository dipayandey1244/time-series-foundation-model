A field guide to models that learn reusable structure

Foundation models are learning to read, see and act in the world.

One family learns from sequences. One recognizes visual structure. A newer family tries to predict how a world changes when an agent acts. Their shared promise is reuse; their evidence and maturity are not the same.

Examples and availability checked October 2026 · deployment claims linked to their publishers.

Three foundation-model families

Different input, different output, different kind of question.

01 · TIME SERIES

Learn from what changes over time

history → future

Pretrained forecasters use past values to predict demand, energy load, traffic or telemetry. The output is a future trajectory, often with uncertainty.

ŷt+1:t+H = fθ(y1:t)

Examples: TimesFM · Chronos · Moirai

02 · VISION

Find structure in images and video

pixels → objects

Promptable vision models detect, segment and track objects. They can become reusable components in editing, visual search, scientific annotation and perception systems.

m̂1:T = fθ(x1:T, q)

Example: Meta Segment Anything (SAM)

03 · WORLD ACTION

Predict what an action will change

scene + action → outcome

World-action models couple a predictive model of physical scenes with action generation. The aim is to anticipate consequences, then plan or control—not just describe pixels.

Where they are showing up

Concrete uses, with the stage of adoption made explicit.

Time seriesForecasting in products and operations
Google Cloud · TimesFM. BigQuery makes a pretrained forecaster available through AI.FORECAST: a team can query timestamped values and get future estimates without first building a separate forecasting model for every series. Google Research reported hundreds of millions of monthly TimesFM queries across BigQuery and AlloyDB in its 2025 review. That is evidence of substantial product use, not a guarantee that every forecast is accurate. BigQuery TimesFM ↗ · Google Research ↗

Decathlon · Chronos-2. The retailer evaluated weekly demand forecasts across product and supply-zone series against its production baseline, then selected Chronos-2 for the forecasting workflow. The practical value is a reusable starting point at retail scale; local data checks and operational monitoring still matter. AWS / Decathlon ↗

In practiceForecasts inform replenishment, inventory and planning. A model’s zero-shot capability can shorten setup, but teams compare it to local baselines and validate errors by product, horizon and season.
VisionRecognition and visual editing
Meta · SAM 3 and SAM 3D. Meta describes the models as part of Facebook Marketplace’s “View in Room” experience. The useful distinction is between seeing an object and making it usable in a scene: SAM 3 identifies and segments prompt-specified objects in images or video; SAM 3D helps produce object-level 3D representations that can support the room-viewing workflow. Meta also worked with Conservation X Labs and Osa Conservation on a wildlife-monitoring video dataset created with SAM 3, showing how segmentation can assist data preparation for ecological research. Meta · SAM 3 ↗
In practiceA prompt turns a general visual model into an object-selection tool; masks and tracks can feed editing, visualization or human-reviewed annotation. This is a reusable perception component, not a replacement for the downstream application or scientific judgment.
World actionRobotics and physical AI
NVIDIA · Cosmos. These systems move from recognizing a scene to asking what might happen next if a robot acts. NVIDIA’s Cosmos 3 Policy DROID demonstrations combine predicted robot action trajectories with future camera views, tying “what should I do?” to “what would I see if I did it?”. A separate Cosmos Edge guide describes post-training a manipulation policy for Jetson Thor, bringing the learned policy toward on-device control. NVIDIA · WAMs ↗ · NVIDIA · robot policy ↗
In practicePredicted futures can help generate training data, compare candidate actions or guide a constrained robot task. These examples are research and developer demonstrations, not evidence of general-purpose autonomous deployment.

A useful distinction: “foundation model” describes a training and reuse strategy, not proof of safe or reliable deployment. TimesFM and vision segmentation already have named product workflows; world-action models are newer and their evidence is mainly research and controlled demonstrations. Test every model on the target task.
